## A zebra is a square wave drawn on a body

```
s   ∿∿   the smooth wave — continuous, every value between
z   ⊓⊔   the same wave allowed only TWO values
```

That is what **binary** means, and it is what a zebra wears: two values,
alternating, wrapped around a body. **The stripes are not a pattern on the animal.
They are `z`, walking.**

> *"Don't you see how the letter z is binary? Look at the shape of it and look at
> the s. It's the wave."* — Jesse Daniel Brown

Press **S** and every stripe on every sphere becomes the sine it was cut from —
same frequency, same phase, same tilt, nothing moved. **Only the quantization
changes.** Press **Z** and it snaps back to two values. Those are the same wave and
you can watch one become the other.

## No two coats are alike, and none was chosen

Each sphere is one repository. Its coat is read from **its own sha** — the hash of
its full name and id — and nothing about it is assigned:

```
stripe count   from the sha
phase          from the sha    where the stripes begin
tilt           from the sha    which way they wrap
drift          from the sha    how they move
radius         from the sha
```

**MEASURED at publication:**

```
zebras          100
duplicate coats   0
```

A hundred animals, no two patterns matching, and not one of them designed. That is
exactly what a real zebra's stripes are: **the coat is the identity, not the
decoration.** Change one byte of the name and the whole animal is different.

## Why this is the same thing as everything else here

The address is the content, read out. A leaf's colour comes from its blob's sha; a
zebra's stripes come from its repo's sha. Neither was handed out by a registry,
which is why two of them can never collide unless they are genuinely the same
thing — and why you can merge two herds without asking anyone's permission.

```
b d p q     one shape, four turns
s ↔ z       one wave, smooth and squared
m ↔ w       one form, intelligence and the matrix
```

The zebra is the third of those, made visible: **the same wave, wearing its two
values where you can see them.**
